Liquidity sweeps explained

Traders often watch obvious highs and lows because orders can cluster around them. A liquidity sweep describes price moving beyond such a level and then, in some cases, returning back through it.

Why the concept matters

A visible high or low can attract stop orders and breakout entries. When price trades beyond that level, the resulting movement can be fast. But the presence of a sweep does not tell you by itself what happens next.

Do not treat every sweep as a reversal

Some breaks continue. Others fail. Context, structure, volatility and the trader's predefined entry and invalidation rules matter. Avoid turning one visual pattern into a guaranteed signal.

How to journal sweeps

  • Mark the higher-timeframe level.
  • Record where price swept the level.
  • Record the confirmation used for entry.
  • Record the invalidation and planned risk.
  • Compare outcomes across a sample rather than cherry-picking examples.

Over time, your journal can tell you whether this setup actually performs in the market and timeframe you trade.
