Trading journal metrics that actually help you improve

A journal becomes more useful when you turn raw trades into a few consistent measurements.

Win rate

Win rate is winning trades divided by closed trades. It is useful, but it cannot be evaluated alone. A strategy with a lower win rate can still have positive expectancy if average winners are sufficiently larger than average losers.

Average win and average loss

Separate winners and losers. Compare their average sizes and watch how they change when you trade different setups or markets.

Expectancy

A simple sample expectancy is total net P&L divided by the number of trades. It tells you the average result per trade in the sample, but costs and sample size matter.

Profit factor

Profit factor is gross profit divided by gross loss. A value above 1 means gross profits exceed gross losses in the sample; it does not guarantee future performance.

R-multiple

R expresses each outcome relative to the amount you planned to risk. +2R means twice the planned risk was gained; -1R means the planned risk was lost. R can make trades easier to compare across position sizes.

Best practice: Segment metrics by setup, direction, market and whether the plan was followed. A strong overall number can hide one setup that is consistently losing.
