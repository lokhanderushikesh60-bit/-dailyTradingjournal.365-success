How to keep a trading journal: a simple step-by-step system

A useful journal is not a diary of every emotion. It is a dataset that helps you test whether your process is repeatable.

1. Record the plan before entry

Write the symbol, direction, setup, entry, invalidation, target and planned risk. Recording these before the outcome reduces hindsight bias.

2. Record what actually happened

After the trade closes, record exit price, result, R-multiple, fees when available and whether the original plan was followed.

3. Record psychology

Use a small set of repeatable labels such as calm, FOMO, fear, greed or revenge. The goal is to discover patterns, not to judge yourself.

4. Review groups of trades

One trade proves very little. Review a meaningful sample and compare setups, days, direction, risk and plan adherence. Look for repeated behavior.

Weekly review questions:
  • Which setup performed best?
  • Where did I break my rules?
  • Did I change size because of emotion?
  • What one behavior should I repeat?
  • What one behavior should I remove?

5. Keep the system easy

If journaling takes too long, consistency suffers. Use a small number of fields and make the review more important than collecting endless notes.
