Position sizing explained

Position sizing answers a practical question: how much quantity can be traded if the stop is hit and you want the loss to stay within a predefined amount?

The basic formula

Position size = money risk ÷ distance from entry to stop

Example

Suppose an account is ₹100,000 and planned risk is 1%. Money risk is ₹1,000. If entry-to-stop distance is ₹2 per unit, the simple position size is 500 units.

Why the stop matters

The stop defines the distance that turns quantity into monetary risk. Choosing quantity first can accidentally create much larger risk than intended.

Real-world adjustments

Fees, slippage, gaps, contract multipliers, lot sizes, leverage and exchange rules can change the actual result. Treat the basic formula as an estimate and verify the instrument specifications.
