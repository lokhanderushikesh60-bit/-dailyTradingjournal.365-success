7 common trading mistakes

Most recurring trading mistakes are process failures that can be measured in a journal.

1. Chasing FOMO

Entering because price is moving is different from entering because your setup triggered. Record the reason for every entry.

2. Revenge trading

Increasing risk to recover a loss quickly can compound the problem. Record risk before entry and compare it with your normal plan.

3. Moving the stop

Moving invalidation farther away changes the original risk. Note every stop adjustment and the reason.

4. Oversizing after wins

A winning streak can create false certainty. Compare position size with your predefined risk rules.

5. Overtrading

More trades do not necessarily mean more opportunity. Track trades per day and whether each matched a valid setup.

6. Ignoring costs

Fees, spreads and slippage can turn a small edge into a poor result. Include costs where your market data allows.

7. Judging yourself from one trade

A single outcome is noisy. Review samples and separate process quality from trade outcome.
