TRADING PSYCHOLOGY

Trading psychology is a process problem, not a motivation problem

Many traders know their rules but break them under pressure. The useful question is not “How do I stay confident?” but “What system makes the right action easier when I am emotional?”

1. FOMO

Fear of missing out often appears after a fast move. A trader sees price accelerate and enters without the original setup. The fix is a predefined entry condition and a rule that a missed trade is acceptable. If the setup is gone, there is nothing to chase.

2. Revenge trading

After a loss, the mind wants to restore the previous account balance quickly. That can lead to larger size, lower-quality setups or repeated entries. A practical safeguard is a loss-triggered pause: stop, record what happened, and only resume when the next valid setup appears.

3. Overconfidence

A streak of winners can be as dangerous as a losing streak. Traders may increase size because recent results feel like proof. Keep risk rules tied to your tested plan rather than to your latest outcome.

4. Use a checklist

Before entering:
  • Is the setup clearly defined?
  • Where is the invalidation?
  • How much money is at risk?
  • Am I entering because of my plan or because price is moving?
  • Will I accept the loss if the stop is hit?

5. Journal the decision, not just the result

A profitable trade can still be a bad process, and a losing trade can be a good process. Record the setup, risk, emotion, plan adherence and lesson. Review a group of trades instead of judging yourself from one result.
