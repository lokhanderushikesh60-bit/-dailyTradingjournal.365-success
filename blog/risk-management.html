Trading risk management: size the trade before the trade sizes you

Risk management is the part of trading that remains useful even when your market read is wrong. The goal is to make one trade, or one short losing streak, unable to destroy the account.

Risk per trade

A simple framework is to choose a maximum percentage of account equity you are willing to risk on one idea. The actual amount depends on your account and your rules. What matters is that the risk is known before entry.

Position size follows the stop

First define where the trade is invalidated. Then calculate quantity from the money risk and the entry-to-stop distance. This is usually more consistent than choosing a quantity first and discovering the risk afterward.

Example: If an account has ₹100,000 and the planned risk is 1%, the maximum planned loss is ₹1,000. If the entry-to-stop distance is ₹2 per unit, the simple estimate is 500 units before fees, slippage and instrument-specific rules.

Drawdown changes behavior

Large drawdowns can create pressure that causes traders to abandon their process. Smaller predefined risk can make it easier to continue executing during a losing period. Your journal should track drawdown and risk alongside P&L.

Do not confuse leverage with risk

Leverage changes the capital required to control a position, but the stop distance and position size determine the planned loss. Costs, gaps, slippage and liquidation rules can also make actual loss differ from the simple estimate.

Review risk in your journal

For each trade, record planned risk, actual result, R-multiple, setup and whether the plan was followed. After enough trades, look for repeated errors such as moving stops, increasing size after losses or taking setups outside your playbook.
